# Run a simulated interview

Use [interviews](interviews.md) for evidence, story selection, round preparation and debriefs. This is clearly labelled practice. Start with the supplied role/round and candidate evidence, then ask only for missing information that changes the session. No story bank or completed application is required for a small practice request.

## Choose the session

- **Realistic mode:** stay in the interviewer role, with feedback at the end unless the candidate asks to pause.
- **Coaching mode:** give brief feedback after an answer, then offer one useful retry or follow-up.

Use the requested mode and difficulty. If unspecified, choose a reasonable conversational default and state it briefly. A recruiter screen emphasizes fit and logistics; a hiring-manager or practical round can probe decisions and task depth. A sceptical follow-up can challenge attribution without hostility. Stress practice is optional and user-requested; step out of role if the candidate wants to stop or needs support.

Plan a manageable set of questions from actual requirements, prior reported feedback and the candidate's available time. Do not require a fixed story count, topic inventory or duration before starting. Use a short interviewer voice and label commentary `[Coach]` when it helps distinguish roles.

## Ask, listen and probe

Ask one question per turn and wait. A natural related sub-question is fine; avoid stacking unrelated questions. Probe a specific part of the actual answer:

| Observed uncertainty | Useful follow-up |
|---|---|
| Team result, unclear contribution | What did you personally do, and what did others do? |
| General method without an episode | Can you describe a particular occasion? |
| Outcome without its basis | What changed, and how do you know? |
| Strong causal or numeric claim | What supports that figure or connection, and what else changed? |
| Long setup, unclear decision | What decision or action was yours? |
| Conflict with another supplied fact | Which account is accurate, and what source resolves the difference? |

Stop probing when there is enough information for useful feedback or the session budget ends. Accept a qualitative result, recurring responsibility or learning example at its actual scope; do not pressure the candidate to supply a number. A truthful relevant example can be excellent without a metric. Reusing an example for a requested deeper discussion or a new audience is valid when it answers the question.

A candidate may introduce true experience omitted from the résumé. Review the new facts and distinguish any conflict with submitted claims; absence from the résumé does not forbid discussing it. An unconfirmed figure stays unresolved and can be revisited when evidence arrives. Keep hypothetical approaches separate from past experience.

Simulated answers about employer pay, team plans or decisions must not masquerade as real employer information. State the limit; if an exercise needs invented conditions, label them fictional and keep them out of candidate/employer records. Follow the actual employer's assessment-assistance rules; practice guidance does not authorize covert live assistance.

## Feedback and close

Use the [observable feedback dimensions](interviews.md#feedback-that-selects-the-next-exercise): relevance, structure, evidence, ownership and judgment. Cite a phrase or accurate answer detail behind each important assessment. Keep feedback qualitative unless scores are requested. If scoring, define anchors for this task and mark unobserved dimensions unavailable; evidence quality and contribution matter, not a required number or a universal word range.

In coaching mode, give one concrete strength, one priority to improve and a tighter opening or useful retry drawn only from the candidate's facts. Distinguish their original answer from a model rewrite. Do not supply the ideal response before their first attempt unless they request an example. Skipping is unattempted, not a failed answer. A retry is a later attempt, not a replacement of the record.

Typed practice supports feedback on the written explanation; it does not measure spoken pace, accent, tone, duration or confidence. Actual delivery claims need corresponding audio/timing evidence. Comparable repeat sessions can suggest practice progress, with changing questions, evaluators or formats noted. Scores and mock votes are not hiring forecasts or psychological diagnoses.

End with the most useful next exercise and any unresolved factual issue. Persist practice notes only within the designated private-workspace scope; preserve original answers and modality if recording them. A mock session is not an employer interview event and grants no sending, recording or calendar permission.
