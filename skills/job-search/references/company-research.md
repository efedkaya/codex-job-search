# Employer research and professional outreach

Use for a requested employer review, a concrete posting concern, interview preparation or a professional outreach draft. Ordinary job assessment needs only enough employer context to change the decision; deeper research is optional. Reuse dated findings when still suitable, and keep the research within the current task's budget. Stop when the material questions are answered or access/budget limits are reached; disclose what remains unknown.

## Research for a decision

Start with the posting and official employer sources. Verify changeable facts live and record the source, publication/event date when available and checked date. An old announcement is not evidence of current strategy or headcount. Use independent reporting where it adds evidence; anonymous reviews and candidate interview reports are attributed experiences, not established employer-wide facts.

Select the axes relevant to this role rather than imposing a technology-company template:

- What the employer sells or delivers, to whom, and what this team is responsible for.
- Recent relevant products, projects, funding, partnerships or organizational changes.
- The actual role's likely problems, success measures and operating constraints.
- Work arrangement, management/support, targets, compensation structure and hiring process.
- Relevant competitors or alternatives when they help explain the employer's choices.

Separate observed facts, candidate-reported information and hypotheses. Connect each useful finding to a candidate evidence pointer or a question to ask. Do not claim an internal problem from marketing copy, invent a future roadmap or infer interviewers' personalities from public biographies. Preserve conflicting dates/entities and ask about the discrepancy rather than silently choosing the more appealing version.

## Posting and recruiter concerns

Apply [search verification](search.md) first. Capture the actual observation: live/closed apply state, redirect target, requisition, visible dates, contradictory terms or repeated appearances with their URLs and observation dates. Title similarity can suggest further checking; it is not proof of identity, reposting, fraud or an unfilled role. Distinct requisitions stay separate. A missing salary, old listing, generic description, funding round or layoff does not alone establish whether a specific opening is genuine or active.

For recruiter-originated opportunities, verify the person's current professional affiliation and the application/contact route through independent official sources where possible. A profile claim or matching display name alone is weak evidence; unresolved identity stays unverified. Requests for candidate payments, unusual financial information or sensitive documents before a verified hiring process warrant a concrete caution and independent verification. Keep this check proportional to the observed issue rather than turning every search into an investigation.

Turn role concerns into specific questions: which entity employs the person, whether a fixed-term role is employee or contractor work, how variable pay is earned, why the role is open, who supports the hire, or what success looks like in the first months. A label such as “contract” does not establish legal classification. Use [offers](offers.md) for written terms and jurisdiction-dependent questions.

## A compact research brief

When useful for an application or interview, assemble a brief about the actual team/business unit: what it does, a few dated source-backed priorities, role-relevant recent developments, candidate evidence and questions to ask. Prefer employer material and filings for their own claims; label news, reviews and inference distinctly. A saved brief retains its checked date and sources. Recheck changeable claims before using them in finished materials rather than treating cached notes as current facts.

Contact before applying can be useful when a named verified contact invites questions or an unresolved material issue deserves one. Draft a substantive question about duties, scope or process; do not make a call mandatory or contact someone merely to create name recognition. Use [messages](messages.md) for the requested draft and preserve the normal sending boundary.

## Contacts and outreach drafts

When contact research or outreach preparation is requested, first use relevant contacts or prior interactions already supplied in the designated workspace. A saved name or shared employer does not prove a relationship, referral or willingness to help. Reverify a stale professional affiliation before relying on it.

Find a small relevant set through public professional sources: an assigned recruiter, a verified team hiring manager, or a peer with a clear connection to the role. State the evidence for the connection; do not identify someone as the hiring manager from title proximity alone. Use source-supported work contact details only; do not guess email addresses or seek personal phone/address information.

Adapt the draft to the recipient: eligibility/logistics for a recruiter, a relevant delivery example for a hiring manager, or a modest role question for a peer. Use one source-backed reason for contacting this person, one defensible candidate proof point and one clear request. Verify the actual channel's current character limit when it matters and count the finished message. Do not hardcode platform limits or imply a personal connection that has not occurred.

Return the contact/source and draft; include an attachment checklist for application emails when useful. Saving a research note is not sending, connecting, requesting a referral or scheduling a follow-up. Follow SKILL.md's action rules.
