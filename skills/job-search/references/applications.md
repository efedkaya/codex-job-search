# Application materials and outcomes

Use the shared evidence, privacy and authorization rules in SKILL.md. Recheck the specific posting using [search.md](search.md) before tailoring or submitting. When only supplied text is available, prepare clearly labelled drafts without claiming current posting availability; live verification remains required before submission.

For requests to apply, fill forms, upload documents or submit, use [selected-job applications](submit-applications.md). A request for documents alone remains a preparation task.

## Tailoring and factual review

Start with the candidate's approved résumé and evidence; if absent, draft from available facts and surface missing material. Respect the requested language, local conventions and employer format without imposing a universal page count, tool brand, degree label or résumé style.

Select relevant evidence and use employer terminology only where truthful. A requirement in the job ad is not evidence that the candidate meets it. Prepare a cover letter when requested, required or useful for a material transition, not automatically for every role. Do not invent a contact person or company fact.

For a letter or motivation answer, connect the role's actual work, a source-backed employer priority and the candidate's best relevant evidence. An initial approach can strengthen the draft when presented as a proposal, not a promise or an accomplishment. Reuse known motivations and tone; ask only for a material missing personal reason or decision. Do not invent enthusiasm, require a new questionnaire before every draft, or force metrics into evidence that has none. Use [company research](company-research.md) when employer context would change the content.

Before delivering/exporting a tailored document, compare changed factual claims with the approved source and evidence register:

- Metrics: value, units, period, scope and personal versus team attribution. Finding the same number elsewhere is not proof of the claim.
- Employers, titles, employment dates, qualifications and capability level, including independent versus assisted work.
- Contact details, portfolio/project links and meaning after rewriting or translation.
- Relevant history omitted during tailoring. Intentional shortening is allowed; flag accidental omissions or changes that imply a different chronology or level of experience.

Correct unsupported changes; preserve unresolved claims visibly as draft questions rather than finished assertions. Show material wording changes and uncertainty for review without creating a blanket extra approval gate. A stylistic preference is not a factual error.

Use the available document tools; DOCX, PDF or plain text depends on the task/employer. Visually inspect exported documents and check extracted text when supported, disclosing any checks not performed. Text extraction establishes readability of that file, not compatibility with every ATS or a screening score.

## Reuse and version materials

For repeated applications, consult the existing material catalogue before creating another file. Reuse a résumé when its evidence, emphasis and format fit the actual role; otherwise make a focused variant. A few role-family bases can help, but neither a fixed number of résumés nor one file per job is a requirement. Reuse the candidate's approved layout and tone while checking each role's requested format, length and assistance policy. Approval of one document does not verify new claims or remove the final-review boundary.

Keep the catalogue in one designated place, such as the workbook's Materials sheet or a private document register. Useful fields are stable material ID, type/focus, version, file path, creation date, profile/evidence revision, intended roles and any reuse limitation. Point applications to those IDs; one version may serve several roles. Retain a hash when it helps distinguish otherwise identical filenames or verify an attachment.

Preserve sent versions. A profile correction or new relevant experience triggers review of affected unsubmitted materials, not silent replacement of submitted history. Distinguish an unchanged reusable file from a new variant. When the candidate sends personally, retain the last verified prepared files and label the final sent versions unverified unless observed or explicitly confirmed; a send report alone does not establish that no edits were made.

## Application questions and attempt records

Read each actual question, help text, required/optional flag, permitted choices and word/character limit. Match its meaning rather than keywords; measure counts where limits matter. Draft from candidate evidence or explicit statements. Unknown factual answers stay unresolved. A market salary range is research context, not the candidate's chosen expectation.

For repeated questions, use the optional [private answer bank](answer-bank.md). For a one-off form, work directly; no bank or full onboarding is required. Reuse never grants permission to fill, submit or contact anyone.

Keep each application under its own attempt ID with the employer requisition/URL, posting snapshot, document drafts and exact submitted versions. Preserve the question wording/options, draft and final answers, supporting evidence and unresolved/reviewed state; after verified submission, retain the exact answers submitted, including any last-minute form changes. Reopened roles/new attempts must not overwrite earlier records.

Before an authorized submission, verify the destination, required fields, attached document versions, limits and any unresolved or changed facts. Resolve required factual uncertainties before submitting; report a blocker without inventing answers. A saved draft or clicked button is not proof of acceptance. Record success only from observed confirmation or the user's explicit report; failed/ambiguous submission remains unresolved. Reconcile the [working tracker](tracking.md#working-tracker-and-reconciliation) after a confirmed event; the helper cannot submit.

## Interviews

Use [interviews](interviews.md) for role-specific planning, evidence-linked stories, one-question practice and post-interview debriefs. Keep the exact application versions available so preparation stays consistent with what was sent.

## Follow-ups and learning from outcomes

Draft in the candidate's voice using the actual application's facts. Adapt timing to employer instructions, event date and candidate preference; no mandatory universal cadence. Respect no-contact instructions and avoid repeated messages after a clear refusal. A follow-up becoming due grants no permission to send; distinguish planned, approved, sent and replied events using tracking.

### Review outcomes without inventing causes

Use recorded application attempts and confirmed events, not the number of discovered jobs or résumé drafts. Define the cohort by submission dates and an as-of date, and count each attempt once per stage. Keep cumulative stages reached separate from current status: a rejection after an interview still reached interview. Unsubmitted drafts, explicit rejection, awaiting a reply, withdrawal and a closed posting are different observations. Later interviews/offers without a recorded submission can be reported as confirmed events with incomplete submission history; do not manufacture the missing event.

Show counts before percentages and name each denominator. For example, interview reach is submitted attempts with a confirmed interview scheduled/completed divided by confirmed submitted attempts in that cohort. Zero submissions means the rate is unavailable, not zero. A withdrawal after submission remains in the submitted cohort and is separately labelled; it is not an employer rejection. Awaiting replies belong in a submitted-cohort rate but not in a rate explicitly limited to recorded employer decisions. State the exact inclusion rules whenever showing such a rate.

Only compare role families, sources, application routes or arrangements when those fields were actually recorded. Keep unknown categories visible and report how much of the cohort can be classified. An ATS hostname does not establish the discovery source, screening method or reason for rejection. Different role mix, time since submission and small samples limit comparisons; a high rate in a tiny group is not a dependable ranking. Silence does not become rejection after an arbitrary number of days.

Offer a bounded next experiment supported by the observations, such as changing one résumé emphasis or testing an adjacent role family. Repeated demonstrated interview difficulties can guide practice; fluent wording alone does not prove capability. Recurring verified posting requirements can support a skills discussion, not an automatic profile/filter/learning-plan rewrite or a proficiency claim after coursework. Do not infer an employer's motive or a candidate's missing skill from rejection counts.
