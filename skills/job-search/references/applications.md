# Applications, interviews and outcomes

## Tailoring

Start from the candidate's approved résumé and evidence. If absent, produce a base draft from available facts and surface missing material. Respect the requested language, local conventions and employer format. Do not impose a universal page count, tool brand, degree label or résumé style.

Select relevant evidence and use the employer's terminology where truthful. Do not insert unsupported keywords, upgrade job titles, turn assisted output into independent engineering, or attribute team revenue to one person. Show material wording changes and unresolved claims for review.

Prepare a cover letter when requested, required, or useful for explaining a material transition. Do not make it a mandatory artifact for every job. Answer requested application questions with measured word/character counts where limits matter. No invented contact person, company fact or résumé metric.

Use the document-generation facilities actually available. DOCX, PDF or plain text are valid depending on the user/employer. Visually inspect exported documents and check extracted text when supported; disclose checks that could not be performed. A text extraction check establishes readability of that file, not a guarantee about all ATS products or a screening score.

Keep private files under an application attempt ID. Record the employer requisition/URL, captured posting, draft versions and exact submitted versions separately. When the same title reopens, create a new attempt; never silently reuse an older posting or overwrite submitted evidence.

If the user asks to submit, apply the host's authorization rules and verify required fields and documents. A draft being saved or a submit button being clicked is not proof of acceptance. Record success only from observed confirmation or the user's explicit report. A failed or ambiguous submission remains unresolved. The optional tracker cannot submit anything.

## Interviews

Use the particular posting and materials for that application attempt. Prepare a small set of likely task-based questions, relevant stories and honest answers about gaps. Separate what the candidate did from team context. Use a situation/action/result structure when helpful, but leave missing facts as questions rather than composing fictional stories.

Practice one question at a time when requested, with feedback on specificity, evidence and relevance. For technical or commercial tasks, explain what the exercise tests and ask the candidate to attempt it; AI producing a solution does not establish their capability. Respect any employer rules on assistance for assessments.

Research company facts from verified public sources. Do not infer interviewers' personality or private attributes. Prepare questions about uncertain responsibilities, support, targets, work arrangement and compensation. Interview preparation should replace lower-priority work when the search budget is tight.

## Follow-ups and learning from outcomes

Draft follow-ups in the candidate's voice using facts from the actual application. Do not introduce new unsupported achievements. Adapt timing to the employer's instructions, event date and candidate preference; there is no universal mandatory cadence. Respect no-contact instructions and avoid repeated messages after a clear refusal.

“Will send,” “approved,” “drafted,” “sent,” and “received a response” are different events. A sent record needs observed evidence or a user report of completed delivery. Never send messages solely because a follow-up is due.

Keep explicit rejection, no response and candidate withdrawal distinct. Do not infer a rejection reason from silence or one small batch. Summarize real counts and denominators; acknowledge small samples and timing differences. An outcome can guide a conversation about strategy, but it does not prove why an employer acted.

If recurring verified requirements suggest a skill gap, report the pattern and available evidence. Preserve any existing learning plan until the user asks to change it. Do not automatically add qualifications or claim proficiency after coursework.
