# Outreach, follow-ups and process messages

Use for a requested networking note, referral ask, recruiter reply, thank-you or application-process message. Follow [company research](company-research.md#contacts-and-outreach-drafts) for recipient verification and [tracking](tracking.md#interpret-replies-before-changing-status) for events. Drafting, approval and actual delivery remain distinct; due dates do not authorize sending or mailbox access.

## Build a useful message

Establish the actual trigger, recipient relationship, channel and purpose from supplied records. Use the materials actually sent, candidate evidence and confirmed conversations. A link to a people search is a search aid, not a found or verified contact. Do not invent shared connections, referrals, praise, interview callbacks or contact details.

Make the context easy to recognize, include the relevant proof or question and end with one clear practical request. Keep the length appropriate to the channel and situation; check and measure a platform limit when it matters. A short friendly note, a detailed technical failure report and a requested formal email need different shapes. Match the candidate's professional voice and the relationship rather than banning normal phrases or requiring multiple variants.

| Situation | Useful content |
|---|---|
| Networking | A source-backed reason for choosing this person, a short background line and a modest request with an easy way to decline |
| Referral | Exact role/link or requisition, a genuine fit point and a forwardable short introduction when useful; use the person's name as a referrer only after agreement |
| After application | Role, actual submission date, a relevant new detail or specific question; respect employer no-contact instructions |
| After interview | Thanks and a callback to what was actually discussed, with a useful clarification if needed; a callback is optional when no notes exist |
| Scheduling/status | The actual invitation or stated timeline, explicit availability/time zone and a precise next step; availability requested is not a confirmed meeting |
| Competing deadline | An accurately described real offer/deadline and a request about process timing; no invented urgency or first-choice claim |
| Broken form, link or scheduler | Requisition, failed step, observed error, date/time zone and relevant retries, followed by a request for a working route or receipt confirmation |
| Missed confirmed call | Confirmed time/channel stated neutrally, then rescheduling availability; do not claim a missed call when the appointment was never confirmed |
| Rejection or withdrawal | A brief factual response, optional narrow feedback question or genuine reason, without pressure or invented promises |

Use [offers](offers.md) for counteroffers, acceptance or decline wording. With an agency, keep the recruiter and hiring employer distinct; an undisclosed client remains unknown.

## Timing and records

Use the employer's stated next step, relevant deadline and candidate's preferences first. When none exists, offer a modest changeable default: a thank-you soon after the conversation, or one application follow-up after roughly a week or two when a relevant verified contact exists. A second message needs a useful reason and appropriate spacing; silence does not establish rejection or permission to try everyone at the employer.

When the candidate asks for an ongoing queue, derive dates from the actual last relevant outbound and inbound event, with the time zone and source. Keep proposed, approved, sent and received events separate. Reconcile only supported event types and existing notes/working views; do not invent a tracker command or schema. Produce the requested draft without extra approval rounds; the actual sending boundary remains the candidate's current instruction.
